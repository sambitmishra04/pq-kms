1. The Problem: Cyber-Physical & Quantum Threats to Biochips

Digital Microfluidic Biochips (DMFBs) manipulate discrete droplets for point-of-care diagnostics, DNA sequencing, and neonatal care via Electrowetting-on-Dielectric (EWOD).

Cyber-Physical Vulnerabilities

  • Actuation Spoofing: Tampering with electrode high-voltage sequences can cause unintended droplet merging, splitting, or sample degradation.
  • Assay Falsification (Ali et al.): In-transit bit manipulation flips glucose diagnostics (e.g. 238 mg/dL altered to 110 mg/dL), causing fatal misdiagnosis.
  • Replay & Rollback: Adversary re-injects historical bioassay readings to mask deteriorating patient vitals.

The Quantum Threat: "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later"

  • Shor's Algorithm: Breaks RSA-2048, ECDH (X25519), and ECDSA in polynomial time $O(\log^3 N)$.
  • Long-Lived Medical Privacy: HIPAA, GDPR, and genetic assays demand confidentiality spanning 30+ years, far exceeding classical crypto lifespan.
  • Resource Constraints: Biochip edge nodes have strict memory, battery, and bandwidth limits that disqualify heavy legacy protocols.

2. PQ-KMS Architecture & Cryptographic Stack

A hybrid zero-retention architecture adhering strictly to NIST post-quantum standards and NIST SP 800-57 key lifecycle recommendations.

Authentication & Signatures

NIST FIPS 204 ML-DSA-65

  • Proof-of-possession registration (Alg 1)
  • Mutual session challenge signing (Alg 2)
  • Digitally signed versioned CRLs (Alg 5)
  • Domain separated: CTX_REG, CTX_SREQ, CTX_REV

Key Encapsulation

NIST FIPS 203 ML-KEM-768

  • Lattice-based Module-LWE security
  • Generates 32-byte high-entropy shared secret
  • Server encapsulation: 0.060 ms
  • Client decapsulation: 0.045 ms

Data Protection & KDF

AES-256-GCM + RFC 5869 HKDF

  • HKDF-SHA256 session key derivation
  • Deterministic 96-bit IV (role || counter)
  • AAD bound: device_id || session_id || counter
  • Hardware accelerated: 3.10 µs per assay
Zero Plaintext Storage Guarantee: All symmetric keys exist strictly in volatile memory. SQLite stores only non-sensitive metadata (UUID, expiry, counter, device status). Key wiping runs on session expiration, revocation, or crash.

Simulated Attack Scenarios & Mitigations

  • Assay Falsification: In-transit bit-flip modifies 238.5 mg/dL to 110 mg/dL. Result: GCM tag validation triggers immediate packet discard.
  • Actuation Path Hijacking: Malicious droplet routing command injected. Result: Denied; counter mismatch and signature validation failure.
  • Telemetry Replay: Adversary retransmits captured benign assay. Result: Dropped due to strict 64-bit monotonic anti-replay counter.
  • Quantum Eavesdropping: Cryptographic hardness confirmed against Shor's lattice sieving.
